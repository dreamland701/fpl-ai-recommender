# FPL project: findings, 23 September to 14 October 2026

Everything below was measured on the machine, CPU unless marked GPU. Points figures are squad points from the decision backtest unless stated. "21 windows" = the fixed-pipeline re-baseline bench (126 backtests, 2022-23 to 2025-26, 4-week windows); "half" = one closed-loop half-season (GW1-19 or GW20-38) with the planner playing chips.

## 1. The short version

- **The forecasting pipeline had real defects and they were fixed.** The 4 October data-contract repair (+191 over 21 windows) and the 13 October double-gameweek pricing fix (+79 to +139 over 21 windows, 1GW-only variant) are the only two changes in three weeks that moved decisions. Both were bugs in how data reached the model, not new signals.
- **No new feature, objective, model class or corpus beat the noise floor.** F7 to F13, decision-weighted loss, trees, band calibration, congestion, injury history: all null or unresolved. The project's ledger of input scale-ups is 0 for 11.
- **The LLM adapter does not improve decisions.** E18/E19/E20/E21 all share one profile: better MAE, a median-seeking shrink, no squad-points gain over classic+F6. The one window every earlier adapter verdict used (GW35-38) was the single window the adapter wins. The adapter stays in shadow, shown side by side each week.
- **RL on the decision layer reached parity with the planner and no further.** G5 to G10: flat, worse, overfit, parity. Chip timing was decomposed and found to be at the forecast-information ceiling: triple captain timing is luck, bench boost timing is the planner rule. The remaining lever is the schedule (double and blank gameweeks), which the DGW fix now prices correctly.
- **Measurement rules changed.** Per-window noise sd 14.9 (anchor tweaks) and 24-40 (adapters); never judge on one window; never promote a cheap proxy; check step-0 generations before an RL run; use the MILP drafter in every backtest.

## 3. Measurement: what the instrument can resolve

- **Noise floor.** Pooled per-window deltas from twelve experiments already judged null give sd 14.9 points per 4-week window. Minimum detectable effect: 51 over 3 windows, 66 over 5, 101 over 12. A 5-point-per-week edge needs about 34 windows.
- **Adapters are noisier.** Adapter-vs-classic deltas have sd 24.3 (raw) and 39.6 (availability routing) because an adapter changes the drafted squad far more. E17's +2.68σ and E18's +2.55σ were overstated about 1.6x.
- **Windows are cheap for anchor tests.** A classic backtest costs about 30 s of CPU, so an anchor change is judged on the 21-window bench, not 5 windows. The 5-window protocol was self-imposed.
- **GW35-38 is unrepresentative.** It was the only window E19 won (+69) while losing GW27-30 (−38) and GW31-34 (−22). Every adapter verdict before 26 September was measured on it alone.
- **Never promote a cheap proxy.** "Mean actual points of the top-11 forecasts" showed +0.69 per pick for F11; the real optimizer gave about zero. Budget, formation, transfer cost and bench absorb ranking gains.
- **Half-season closed loops are noisier still.** Per-half sd about 65-70 points; a +20/half chip edge needs ten or more held-out halves.
- **Gate bugs fixed twice.** The mechanism checker conflated "signal absent from prompts" with "signal ignored", and its injury regex matched words inside attached news text.
- **RL hygiene.** Always sample step-0 generations and check parse rate and reward spread before a long run (G5 take 2 had 0/8 parses and a flat −5 reward). Check the baseline's realism (the G5 env planner transferred every week and was a strawman).
- **Drafter artifact.** The beam drafter fills GK → DEF → MID → FWD under the 3-per-club cap, so correctly priced Liverpool doubles drafted three defenders and locked Salah out (−97 in one window). Every future backtest uses the exact wildcard MILP draft (`FPL_DRAFT_MILP=1`).

Four retrains, one profile: a little more accurate, no decision gain. Stop retraining this recipe on input changes.

### Why it forecasts better and picks worse
- **Median estimator.** Token cross-entropy plus greedy decoding is median-seeking. Top band: conditional mean 4.58, median 3.0, classic 4.79, E20 3.86. MAE improves; expected points are biased down 0.3-0.85 per game.
- **Shrink.** Mean delta −0.48, corr(delta, baseline) −0.66 in every adapter. Shrink-only recovers ~90% of the MAE gain and backtests identical to classic.
- **Written ≠ believed.** The greedy delta is sign first (P(cut) 0.5-0.65) then the most likely size given a cut; the 35-50% chance of a rise is dropped. Groß written −1.2, expected −0.34; Rogers −1.1 written, +0.05 expected.
- **Expectation decoding** recovers 41 of the 44 points greedy loses (992 vs classic 995 over 4 windows), best RMSE (2.972 vs 3.025 classic), bias +0.02. Still no edge over classic. The weekly sheet now shows classic, SFT as written, and SFT average.
- **Captain pick** was never better than classic's (0/12 weeks).

### What text is worth
- **Availability, not points.** Availability AUC classic → SFT: no article 0.635 → 0.700; Guardian team news 0.638 → 0.754; Scout rows 0.608 → 0.778; price-change and transfer articles make it worse. Points: SFT player-specific signal correlates +0.16 with real surprises with no article, +0.23 with team news, within CIs.
- **Timing.** Guardian team news publishes Friday ~17:00 UTC, 80% inside the last 24 h; removing those articles leaves squad points unchanged (741 vs 736) and costs only availability AUC (+0.105 → +0.040). The 12 h cutoff is cheap.
- **Scout edge faded.** Scout-mentioned players beat classic by +0.72/wk in 2022-23, +0.09 in 2025-26, −0.11 live. Classic prices it via ownership and transfer-trend features.
- **Classic covers the text.** Keyword signals add nothing to points in any season (RMSE −0.008 to +0.002); injury/doubt/suspension words add +0.001 to +0.013 AUC.
- **31B expert reader (untrained, 1,328 article rows).** Outlook rating vs real surprise beyond classic: corr +0.038 ± 0.054, null. start_risk: AUC 0.629 vs classic P(appear) 0.478 on 2025-26, but 0.466 vs 0.675 on 2026-27 (only 8 non-plays). Consistent with text as an availability reader.
- **Non-PL games.** The European fixture file is already in prompts and congestion features; schedule effects measured null. The only unexploited channel is what happened in those games (knocks, minutes), an availability signal that arrives through team-news articles.

### Live standing
Classic+F6 decides. The adapter runs in shadow with a promotion rule (rolling 4-week live availability AUC ≥ +0.03 over classic). Live AUC so far: 0.673 vs 0.673 (E11-full), 0.683 → 0.692 (E20). Never apply E11-full to fixed baselines (−55 on live GW2-5).

## 6. Decision layer and RL

### Planner and optimizer

Fixes the candidate pruning the review flagged; now also the backtest drafter. - **Transfer discipline is already right.** The engine makes 59 transfers in 84 weeks; requiring a minimum projected gain of 1/2/3/5 scores +7/−21/−59/−108.
- **Wildcard-then-bench-boost sequencing.** Rebuilding the wildcard squad to value the bench doubles the following bench boost (8.3 vs 4.3) and adds +4.7/half (se 2.8, 8/12 halves). Wired into the live chip line.

### Chip timing, decomposed
- **Exact tables** (12 halves, every WC×FH pair, TC/BB additive): planner 12,778; hindsight all-four ceiling +1,574 (+131/half) but TC+BB-only hindsight +303 (+25/half). The rest is WC/FH picked knowing outcomes, mostly winner's curse over 381 noisy trajectories.
- **Random-week chip value.** WC −0.9 (range −72..+86 by half, pays only when the squad is weak); FH +2.9.
- **Tiny numeric policy** (1.4k params, LOSO): −71, 6/6, one −149 blow-up. v2 with squad-health features: +93 but 6/6 split and still plays BB in week 2.
- **Captaincy room.** Hindsight best-of-top-3 +60/half, but forecast-best is best-of-3 only 43% of weeks (random 33%): noise-sized.
- **G10 (per-decision, LOSO).** TC: hindsight 19.4, planner 5.2, oracle knowing every future week's forecast 6.4, random 7.0; captain forecast between-week sd 1.3 vs actual 5.5, corr +0.16. Luck. BB: hindsight 20.4, planner 11.0, random 6.7; forecast corr +0.40 pooled but LOSO R² ≤ 0 for every model; learned stopping policies 6.3-11.5. The planner's "play when this week's projection beats the next four" is at the information ceiling for both chips.
- **Wes's direction (10-13):** split chip timing into the noise-free constraint logic (already exact in the planner) and forecasting; train on per-decision table values, not half totals; forecasts are a baseline to subtract, never a reward.
- **Standing:** chip-timing learning closed. Chip advice = planner rule + schedule rule (hold TC/BB for the biggest double ≥6 teams, FH in the biggest blank; earlier measured ~+5 and ~+14 per half when such weeks exist) + G8 compact as a shadow line.

## 7. Double-gameweek pricing defect (13-14 October)

**Finding.** On the 15 double gameweeks in the states (2020-21 to 2025-26), regular starters who doubled had P(appear) 0.56 versus 0.84 the week before, and forecast 4.3 versus actual 5.9 (singles 2.7 / 2.7). The previous week's single-fixture forecast × 2 = 6.2 matched. Cause: `predict_frame` computed P(appear) as minutes / (90 × fixture_count), so ~100 predicted minutes over two games gave 0.56; and the points heads saw a double only as an additive flag, pricing a 7-point player's double at ~8, not 14. Downstream: soft availability discounted every doubler, chip pricing under-valued TC/BB in exactly the weeks hindsight picks them, and the planner fired them in GW20-24 instead.

**Fix** (`FPL_PER_FIXTURE=1`, with tests): minutes and 1GW heads fit on label / fixture_count and multiplied back; P(appear) = clip(minutes per fixture / 90), 0 for blanks. Both training tables gained `fixtures_next_3/5` (forward fixture counts, rate-equivalent like the labels, live from the fixture API), and with them the 3GW/5GW heads go per fixture too unless `FPL_PER_FIXTURE_HORIZON=0`.

**Calibration.** Doublers: P(appear) 0.80, forecast 6.11 vs actual 5.91. Singles and normal weeks unchanged. Forecast-best captain in DGWs: forecast 13.94 vs actual 12.40 (was 8.08 / 11.47). 3-week forecast with a double ahead 11.6 vs 11.7 actual (was 8.9); with a blank ahead 6.1 vs 5.4 (was 8.6).

**Decision backtests, 21 windows (baseline beam 5,162 / MILP draft 5,184).**

| Variant | Beam draft | MILP draft |
|---|---|---|
| P(appear) formula only | +33 | |

The horizon-aware 3/5GW heads are calibrated but decision-neutral and twitchy: the 3-5 week objective sells or skips a premium before a single blank (Salah never drafted in GW12-15 2024-25, −38).

**Closed-loop chip tables, 12 halves, planner Δ vs base (final, 14 October).**

| Variant | Planner Δ | Halves up/down | Mean/half (sd) | No-chips Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1GW-only | +47 | 7/4 | +3.9 (9.5), t ≈ 1.4 | +30 |

The full variant's half-season gain is almost all transfers and captaincy over the long horizon (no-chips +301), with big swings (−71 to +135 by half). It conflicts with the flat 4-week result, so it is unproven, not disproven. In both variants the planner still fires TC/BB in GW20-26 because its 4-week lookahead cannot see GW29-37 doubles; the schedule rule stays necessary. Hindsight all-chips rises in DGW halves (1,306 → 1,427, 1,284 → 1,321), confirming the chip prize lives there.

**Recommendation.** Ship the 1GW-only variant (`FPL_PER_FIXTURE=1 FPL_PER_FIXTURE_HORIZON=0`) for live decisions: it wins on both benches with low variance. Keep the full variant as the candidate to resolve with more halves. Not yet wired into the weekly scripts. Next steps in the DGW engine: chip choice between doubles (TC in the double with the best captain, BB where the squad holds most doublers), building toward the BB week with transfers or a wildcard 2-4 weeks ahead, FH in the biggest blank or in a double with few doublers.

## 9. Rules carried forward

1. Judge anchor changes on the 21-window bench with the MILP drafter; judge adapters on ≥3 windows, never GW35-38 alone; report sigma and say "unresolved" rather than "flat".
2. Test the causal claim on CPU before building the feature, and run the squad backtest before believing any proxy.
3. Classic+F6 decides; the adapter is shown, not used, until the live promotion rule fires.
4. Never quote a written SFT delta without its expected value.
5. Chip advice is the planner rule plus the schedule rule; do not spend GPU on chip-timing RL.
6. Check step-0 generations, the baseline's realism and held-out halves before reading any RL result.
7. Merge LoRA adapters for inference; run long jobs as memory-capped systemd units with absolute paths; at most one 31B alongside training and ≤3 backtest workers (RAM hit 116/119 GB on 10-11).
8. GPU runs need Wes's explicit go-ahead; publish code only.
